# To review: pt commentary batch 006

### 154 Gen 39:7-12 Joseph resists temptation.
- flag: Source reads "by avoiding the temper", evidently a misprint for "tempter"; translated as "evitando a tentadora" (Potiphar's wife).
- flag: Allusions translated in Henry's wording, with Bíblia Livre words where they fit: Jó 31:1 ("pacto com meus olhos"), Gn 39:9 ("grande mal"; KJV "great wickedness"), Rm 7:13 ("excessivamente pecaminoso"), Hb 12:1, Pv 6:5 (the Bíblia Livre has "como a corça do caçador, como o pássaro do caçador de aves"; Henry's "bird from the snare" kept).

### 156 Gen 39:19-23 He is cast into prison, God is with him there.
- quoted (Gen 39:21): “the Lord was with Joseph” → “o SENHOR foi com José, e estendeu a ele sua misericórdia”
- quoted (Gen 39:21): “gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison” → “deu-lhe favor aos olhos do chefe da casa do cárcere”
- flag: unlisted allusions: Hb 12:2 ("olhar para Jesus"; the Bíblia Livre has "olhando para Jesus"), Hb 4:15 ("sem pecado"); translated.

### 157 Gen 40:1-19 The chief butler and baker of Pharaoh in prison, Their dreams interpreted by Joseph.
- quoted (Ps 42:5): “Why art thou cast down, O my soul” → “Minha alma, por que tu estás abatida”

### 158 Gen 40:20-23 The ingratitude of the chief butler.
- flag: unlisted quotations in the Bíblia Livre's words: Gn 40:23 ("não se lembrou de José, ao invés disso lhe esqueceu"), Dt 32:6 ("povo tolo e insensato"); Ec 7:1 echoed ("melhor que o dia do nosso nascimento").

### 159 Gen 41:1-8 Pharaoh's dreams.
- flag: Gn 41:8: KJV "his spirit was troubled"; the Bíblia Livre has "estava movido seu espírito"; translated.

### 160 Gen 41:9-32 Joseph interprets Pharaoh's dreams.
- quoted (Gen 41:17): “stood upon the bank of the river” → “estava à beira do rio”
- quoted (Isa 56:12): “to-morrow shall be as this day” → “o dia de amanhã será como hoje”
- translated, not quoted (Ex 16:18; Henry turns Êx 16:18 into a present-tense rule ("he that gathers much, has nothing over"); the Bíblia Livre's past narrative ("não sobrava ao que havia recolhido muito") doesn't fit his sentence, and the listed run is only a fragment.): “nothing over; and he”
- flag: Is 56:12 ("o dia de amanhã será como hoje") is the drunkards' boast in the verse; Henry borrows the words for his own point.

### 161 Gen 41:33-45 Joseph's counsel, He is highly advanced.
- translated, not quoted (Gen 41:38, Gen 41:39; Henry turns Pharaoh's question ("em quem haja espírito de Deus?") into a statement; the Bíblia Livre's subjunctive can't stand there.): “is a man in whom the spirit of God”
- flag: Zaphnath-paaneah given as the Bíblia Livre spells it ("Zefenate-Paneia", Gn 41:45).

### 162 Gen 41:46-57 Joseph's children, The beginning of the famine.
- flag: unlisted quotations in the Bíblia Livre's words: Ec 9:10 ("tudo quanto vier à tua mão para fazer, faze"), Gn 42:36 ("José desapareceu"; KJV "Joseph is not"), Jo 2:5 ("fazei tudo quanto ele vos disser"), Sl 107:9 ("encherá de bem a alma faminta", future for the verse's past), Is 55:1 ("sem dinheiro e sem preço").

### 164 Gen 42:7-20 Joseph's treatment of his brethren.

### 165 Gen 42:21-24 Their remorse, Simeon detained.

### 166 Gen 42:25-28 The rest return with corn.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Is 55:1 in the Bíblia Livre's words ("sem dinheiro e sem preço").

### 167 Gen 42:29-38 Jacob refuses to send Benjamin to Egypt.
- quoted (Gen 42:36): “All these things are against me” → “contra mim são todas estas coisas”
- quoted (Gen 42:36): “all these things are against” → “todas estas coisas”
- flag: echoes of Rm 8:28, 2 Co 4:17, 1 Pe 2:3 and 1 Pe 5:6 ("debaixo da sua poderosa mão", as the Bíblia Livre has it) in Henry's wording.

### 168 Gen 43:1-14 Jacob is persuaded to send Benjamin into Egypt.

- flag: Fp 3:8: Henry's "knowledge of Jesus Christ" is the Bíblia Livre's "conhecer a Cristo Jesus".

### 171 Gen 44:1-17 Joseph's policy to stay his brethren, and try their affection for Benjamin.
- quoted (Gen 44:5): “Is not this it in which my lord drinketh” → “Não é isto no que bebe meu senhor”
- flag: Gn 44:5: Henry's two readings ("for which he would search thoroughly" / "make trial") explain the KJV's "whereby indeed he divineth"; the Bíblia Livre has "pelo que costuma adivinhar"; translated as Henry gives them.

### 172 Gen 44:18-34 Judah's supplication to Joseph.

- flag: unlisted quotation of Is 53:12 in the Bíblia Livre's words ("intercedeu pelos transgressores").

### 173 Gen 45:1-15 Joseph comforts his brethren, and sends for his father.

### 175 Gen 45:25-28 Jacob receives the news of Joseph's being alive.

### 181 Gen 47:13-26 Joseph's dealings with the Egyptians during the famine.
- quoted (Job 2:4): “All that a man hath will he give for his life” → “tudo quanto o homem tem, dará por sua vida”
- quoted (Gen 47:25): “Thou hast saved our lives” → “A vida nos deste”
- flag: unlisted quotations in the Bíblia Livre's words: Fp 3:8 ("todas as coisas como perda"), Mt 19:29 ("cem vezes tanto").
- flag: Gn 47:25: Henry's "Thou hast saved our lives" is the Bíblia Livre's "A vida nos deste"; his parallel "Thou hast saved our souls" translated.

### 182 Gen 47:27-31 Jacob's age. His desire to be buried in Canaan.
- quoted (Gen 47:29): “the time drew nigh that Israel must die” → “achegaram-se os dias de Israel para morrer”
- quoted (Hos 12:4): “had power over the Angel, and prevailed” → “lutou com o anjo, e prevaleceu”
- quoted (Gen 47:31): “Israel bowed himself upon the bed's head” → “Israel se inclinou sobre a cabeceira da cama”
- flag: Os 12:4: Henry's "had power over the Angel" is the Bíblia Livre's "lutou com o anjo".
- flag: Hb 11:21 in the Bíblia Livre says Jacob worshipped "apoiado sobre a ponta do seu cajado" (staff), not the bed's head; Henry's "as it is explained" kept.
