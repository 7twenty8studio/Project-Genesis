# To review: es commentary batch 032

### 1240 Neh 11:1-36 The distribution of the people.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Phil 2:21: RV1909 'buscan lo suyo propio, no lo que es de Cristo' used verbatim (RV ends 'Cristo Jesús').
- flag: Echo of 2 Co 5:8 ('absent from the body… present with the Lord') translated, since the RV1909 ('partir del cuerpo, y estar presentes al Señor') words it differently.

### 1241 Neh 12:1-26 The priests and Levites that returned.
- flag: Echo of He 13:7 (RV1909 'Acordaos de vuestros pastores, que os hablaron la palabra de Dios'), put in Henry's first person plural and translated.

### 1244 Neh 13:1-9 Nehemiah turns out the mixed multitude.
- flag: The source opens this entry with the next section's heading and verses ('Sabbath-breaking restrained.' / '(15-22)'); kept as given, translated.

### 1245 Neh 13:10-14 Nehemiah's reform in the house of God.
- quoted (Neh 13:10): “had not been given them” → “no se les habían dado”
- quoted (Neh 13:14): “Wipe not out my good deeds” → “no raigas mis misericordias”
- flag: Quote 1: the RV1909 says "no raigas mis misericordias" (raer, to wipe out; "misericordias" = the KJV's "good deeds"); the next sentence keeps "buenas obras" for Henry's own "good deeds".
- flag: unlisted quotation of Neh 13:14: "Acuérdate de mí" (RV1909) for "Remember me".

### 1246 Neh 13:15-22 
- flag: Henry opens with 'the Lord's day' while speaking of Nehemiah's sabbath; rendered 'el día del Señor' as he says, and 'sabbath' as 'sábado'.

### 1247 Neh 13:23-31 The dismissal of strange wives.
- quoted (Neh 13:29): “Remember them, O my God” → “Acuérdate de ellos, Dios mío”
- quoted (Neh 13:31): “Remember me, O my God, for good” → “Acuérdate de mí, Dios mío, para bien”
- flag: Source: 'on this occasion He prays' lacks a full stop, and ends 'greatness of they mercy' (for 'thy'); both read as meant.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Neh 13:22: 'perdóname según la muchedumbre de tu misericordia' (RV1909), with Henry's 'O my God' ('Dios mío', also in the verse) set after 'Perdóname'.
- flag: Echo of Job 42:6 (RV1909 'me aborrezco, y me arrepiento en el polvo y en la ceniza') put in the first person plural.

### 1248 Esther 1:1-9 The royal feast of Ahasuerus.
- flag: Echo of Pr 15:17 ('Better is a dinner of herbs') translated in Henry's own words ('with quietness'); the RV1909 reads 'Mejor es la comida de legumbres donde hay amor'.

### 1250 Esther 2:1-20 Esther chosen queen.
- flag: sensitive: Henry calls the Persian court's marriage customs 'absurd practices' of those 'destitute of Divine revelation'.

### 1251 Esther 2:21-23 Mordecai discovers a plot against the king.
- translated, not quoted (1Thess 1:3; The RV1909 has 'la obra de vuestra fe, y del trabajo de amor' (second person, genitive construction); Henry speaks of 'their' work, so the phrase is translated.): “work of faith and labour of love”
- flag: unlisted quotation of He 6:10: 'Dios no es injusto para olvidar' (RV1909) used verbatim.

### 1252 Esther 3:1-6 Haman seeks to destroy the Jews.
- flag: Echo of Sal 15:4 (RV1909 'Aquel á cuyos ojos es menospreciado el vil') translated around Henry's sentence.

### 1255 Esther 4:5-17 Esther undertakes to plead for the Jews.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Ro 4:18: 'creyó en esperanza contra esperanza' (RV1909) used verbatim; 'staggered not at the promise' (Ro 4:20) translated.
- flag: Henry addresses Esther in the second person ('thou art bound…'), paraphrasing Mordecai; rendered with 'tú' (estás obligada, fuiste elevada).

### 1256 Esther 5:1-8 Esther's application received.
- quoted (Matt 7:7): “Ask, and it shall be given you” → “Pedid, y se os dará”
- quoted (1John 2:1): “we have an Advocate with the Father” → “abogado tenemos para con el Padre”
- flag: unlisted quotation of He 4:16: 'Lleguémonos pues confiadamente al trono de la gracia' (RV1909) used verbatim.
- flag: Ap 22:17 ('the Spirit says, Come, and the Bride says, Come') follows Henry's split of the sentence; the RV1909 reads 'el Espíritu y la Esposa dicen: Ven', so its words 'Ven' and 'Esposa' are kept.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Lc 18:1: 'orar siempre … y no desmayar' (RV1909).

### 1258 Esther 6:1-3 Providence recommends Mordecai to the king's favour.
- flag: Source: 'The king could not sleep when Providence had a design to serve' rendered 'porque' (the evident sense).

### 1260 Esther 6:12-14 Haman's friends tell him of his danger.
- flag: Echo of Job 16:2 (RV1909 'consoladores molestos sois todos vosotros') put in the third person, as Henry has it.

### 1261 Esther 7:1-6 Esther accuses Haman.
- flag: unlisted quotation of Mt 10:28: 'puede destruir el alma y el cuerpo en el infierno' (RV1909; the KJV and Henry say 'body and soul').

### 1262 Esther 7:7-10 Haman hanged on his own gallows.
- quoted (Esther 7:10): “Then was the king's wrath pacified” → “apaciguóse la ira del rey”

### 1263 Esther 8:1-2 Mordecai is advanced.
- flag: Echo of Sal 39:6 (KJV 'he heapeth up riches, and knoweth not who shall gather them') translated; the RV1909 reads 'junta, y no sabe quién lo allegará'.

### 1264 Esther 8:3-14 Esther makes suit for the Jews.
- flag: sensitive: Henry says the unalterable law shows the 'pride and folly' of the Medes and Persians.
- flag: sensitive: the closing question (God's oracles 'locked up… in an unknown tongue') reads as Henry's Protestant rebuke of Scripture and worship kept in Latin; translated as written.
- flag: Echo of Gn 3:5 (RV1909 'seréis como dioses') given in Henry's first person plural, 'Seremos como dioses'.

### 1266 Esther 9:1-19 The success of the Jews.
- quoted (Esther 9:1): “The enemies of the Jews hoped to have power over them” → “esperaban los enemigos de los Judíos enseñorearse de ellos”
- flag: Source typo 'wordly wealth' read as 'worldly'.
- flag: Echo of Fil 1:27 (RV1909 'estáis firmes en un mismo espíritu, unánimes combatiendo juntamente') adapted to Henry's exhortation.

### 1267 Esther 9:20-32 The feast of Purim in remembrance of this.
- flag: sensitive: 'the superstitions of the heathen' (the Persians' casting of lots).
- flag: 'a lot' rendered 'suerte', the RV1909's word for Pur in Est 3:7 and 9:24.

### 1269 Job 1:1-5 The piety and prosperity of Job.
- quoted (Matt 19:24): “a rich man to enter into the kingdom” → “entrar un rico en el reino”
- flag: Quote 0: the RV1909 (Mt 19:24) says 'reino de Dios'; Henry says 'kingdom of heaven', kept as 'de los cielos' after the quoted words.

### 1271 Job 1:13-19 The loss of Job's property, and the death of his children.
- flag: The source ends with '(Job 1:20-22)', the next section's verses; kept as given.

### 1272 Job 1:20-22 Job's patience and piety.
- quoted (1Tim 6:7): “it is certain we can carry nothing out” → “sin duda nada podremos sacar”
- quoted (Neh 9:33): “done right, but we have done wickedly” → “mas nosotros hemos hecho lo malo”
- flag: Quote 1: the RV1909's 'rectamente has hecho' is put in Henry's third person ('Dios ha hecho rectamente'); the rest of his run is the verse verbatim.
- flag: unlisted quotation of 1 Jn 3:8: 'para deshacer las obras del diablo' (RV1909).
- flag: Echo of Job 1:22 (RV1909 'ni atribuyó á Dios despropósito alguno') for 'charge God with folly'.

### 1273 Job 2:1-6 Satan obtains leave to try Job.
- flag: The source repeats the title of section 1270 here ('Satan obtains leave to try Job.'); translated the same.

### 1274 Job 2:7-10 Job's sufferings.
- flag: unlisted quotation of 1 P 5:6: 'bajo la poderosa mano de Dios' (RV1909).
- flag: sensitive: Job's wife 'spared to him, to be a troubler and tempter to him'.

### 1275 Job 2:11-13 His friends come to comfort him.
- quoted (Job 2:13): “they sat down with him” → “se sentaron con él”
- flag: Source garbled: 'we must look life' (words seem missing, perhaps 'look further' or 'look upon it as a duty of life'); translated literally as 'debemos mirar la vida'.
- flag: 'vailed' (= veiled) rendered 'encubierto'.

### 1276 Job 3:1-10 Job complains that he was born.
- flag: Source typo 'consolidation' read as 'consolation' ('consuelo').

### 1277 Job 3:11-19 Job complaining.
- quoted (Job 3:17): “There the wicked cease from troubling” → “Allí los impíos dejan el perturbar”
- flag: The second half of Job 3:17, 'There the weary are at rest', is not listed; given in the RV1909's words, 'allí descansan los de cansadas fuerzas'.
